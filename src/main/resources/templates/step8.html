Step 8: The pipeline, for real

Step 7's pipeline with what a real application needs. The job's state lives in the database, and the stream only reports what's there. Batches are transactions and get retried, the database gets at most a few batches at a time, and a job can be cancelled or time out. A job that doesn't finish leaves no records behind.

What to notice

  • Reload the page while a job runs, or open it in a second tab: the job is still there with its current progress. The address bar has ?job=…, and the stream reads the state from the database.
  • Six batches, but only three insert at once. The others wait. Start two jobs in two tabs: they share the same three slots, because the limit protects the database, not one job.
  • "Fails once": batch 3's transaction rolls back and the batch starts again (try 2). The job still ends with exactly 600 records.
  • "Always fails": after the second try the job asks every other batch to stop, waits until they have, then deletes every record it inserted.
  • "Slow database" runs into the 20 s timeout. Cancel does the same thing on demand. Both only ask the threads to stop; they notice between chunks.
  • Log in as bob and open alice's ?job=… link: it isn't found. Jobs belong to their owner.
